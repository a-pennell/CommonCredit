Housing-First Scale-Up
Drafted by D. Okonkwo Jun 2, 2026 Linked to: Housing-first proposal
Scenario Narrative

In this scenario, the city successfully implements the housing-first encampment response protocol. The 72-hour outreach window becomes standard practice, and DPH secures emergency funding to expand outreach capacity by 40% within six months. SFPD enforcement actions are systematically conditioned on DPH sign-off, and placement rates climb from the current 31% to above 60% within 12 months.

The reinforcing loop between clearings and displacement is broken: individuals who receive confirmed housing placements no longer cycle back to street encampments, reducing the encampment population by an estimated 20–30% over 18 months. Health system costs plateau and begin to decline by year 2 as preventive placements replace emergency interventions.

Equity & Distributional Implications
Who benefits
Unhoused individuals receive confirmed placements before displacement — reduced trauma and continuity of care.
Public Health system: reduced ER admissions and 30-day readmissions over 18-month horizon.
Downtown businesses: gradual reduction in visible encampments tied to housing capacity, not arbitrary enforcement.
Who bears costs / risks
DPH absorbs significant operational and staffing burden — capacity expansion requires funding not yet secured.
Downtown businesses face a longer timeline to visible change compared to enforcement-first scenarios.
City budget: short-term cost increase before health system savings materialise (12–18 month lag).
